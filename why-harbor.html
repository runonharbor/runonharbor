Why We Built Harbor

We were trying to play a clean game of telephone — digitally.

Harbor wasn't born from a software idea. It was built from a problem we lived every day while trying to grow CSC.

As CSC grew, we realized we were spending more time managing information than serving customers.

Every order generated emails, phone calls, supplier conversations, quotes, tracking numbers, notes, and follow-up tasks.

A customer would contact us looking for a specific part. To help them, we would often reach out to multiple suppliers at the same time to compare pricing and availability.

In other cases, we would sell a product and immediately begin coordinating with suppliers to fulfill the order and ensure it shipped on time.

Before long, it felt like we were trying to play a clean game of telephone — digitally.

Information was constantly being passed from customer to supplier, supplier to employee, employee to spreadsheet, spreadsheet to email, and back again.

Every handoff created another opportunity for something to be missed, delayed, or forgotten.

It wasn't that the information didn't exist.

It was that the information lived everywhere.

As the business grew, keeping everyone aligned became exhausting and overwhelming.

We found ourselves constantly switching between emails, spreadsheets, notes, supplier portals, customer records, and order systems just to figure out what needed attention next.

That's when we realized the problem wasn't our people or our process.

The problem was that there wasn't a central place where everything could come together.

That's Why We Built Harbor

Harbor was created to give growing businesses a single place to manage customers, suppliers, orders, assets, documents, projects, and workflows.

A place where information stays connected instead of getting buried across inboxes and spreadsheets.

A place where teams can stay aligned without endless follow-up.

A place where business owners can spend less time chasing information and more time serving customers.

Because growing a business is hard enough.

Keeping it organized shouldn't be.
